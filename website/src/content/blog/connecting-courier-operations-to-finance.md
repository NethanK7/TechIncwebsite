Courier and delivery operators tend to accumulate systems in the order problems arrive, not in the order a plan would choose. A delivery app gets adopted because deliveries need managing today. An accounting package gets adopted separately because invoices need raising today. A website gets built when customers start asking where their parcel is. None of these decisions were wrong at the time — but three years later, the business is running three systems that were each the right call in isolation and don't add up to one operational picture.

[DRH Courier Express](/case-studies/drh-courier-express) was in exactly that position: legacy systems with courier activity separated from invoicing and finance, a mobile application that no longer served the delivery operation, no way for customers to track a parcel online, and inbound inquiries with no structured home.

## Why this is a platform decision, not a feature request

"Integrate the courier app with the accounting system" sounds like an API project. It rarely is, once you look closely — because the underlying systems are usually different generations of technology built for different problems, and forcing them to share data reliably means one of them has to become the source of truth for the other. In DRH's case, the more direct path was retiring the legacy stack for one cloud-based ERP platform, and rebuilding the delivery app and public website against it rather than trying to keep three separate systems synchronised.

That decision touched four things at once:

- **Courier operations integrated directly with invoicing and finance** — a completed delivery becomes the event that drives billing, not a separate reconciliation step.
- **A modern delivery app** replaced the mobile application that no longer served the operation.
- **A new public website with parcel tracking**, developed and integrated within a short timeframe.
- **Customer inquiries routed into the CRM**, so engagement has a structured, single home instead of an inbox nobody owns.

## What "for the first time" actually means here

DRH's own description of the outcome is precise: for the first time, courier operations are seamlessly integrated with invoicing and finance, giving better visibility, control and efficiency across the organisation. That phrase — *for the first time* — is doing real work. It means the gap wasn't a rough edge in an otherwise-connected business; it was the default state, and closing it changed what the business could actually see about itself day to day.

If your operational systems and your finance systems currently agree only after somebody spends a day reconciling them, that reconciliation is the cost — and it is usually a sign the two were never meant to be separate in the first place.
