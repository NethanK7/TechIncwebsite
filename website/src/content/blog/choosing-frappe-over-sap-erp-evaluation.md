When a Tier-1 platform like SAP is genuinely on the shortlist, the business asking the question is usually past the point of comparing licence fees. What they are actually evaluating is whether one connected platform can replace a set of departmental tools that each work fine on their own but don't add up to one operational picture.

[Electro-Serv Lanka](/case-studies/electro-serv-lanka) is a distributor and service provider for electrical, control and automation principals — Schneider Electric, SMC, Hensel, Foxtam Controls, Toho, Line Seiki and HPL among them. Distribution, manufacturing, warehousing and field service all needed to run under one roof. Tier-1 options, SAP included, were formally evaluated before Frappe was selected.

## Why breadth is the risk, not the fit

A six-module footprint — CRM, Finance, Fixed Assets, Supply Chain, Manufacturing and Warehouse Management — is exactly the kind of scope that makes a big-bang go-live dangerous. Segregating a footprint that wide into deliverables with their own acceptance gates is what let the business adopt each area in turn, rather than absorbing the whole system at once. That's the NXTGEN methodology doing its actual job: not speed for its own sake, but turning one enormous risk into six manageable ones.

## What changed, in the business's own terms

Sales process efficiency improved. Inventory visibility improved. Financial transactions run through the system rather than around it — which is the quieter but more important shift, since a transaction that runs around the system is one nobody can report on later.

The clearest signal, though, is commercial rather than technical: five years after commencing in 2021, the client is upgrading to the latest platform version, with the work already underway. Nobody upgrades a system they are quietly planning to replace.

## The actual question to ask

If SAP or another Tier-1 platform is on your shortlist alongside Frappe, the useful comparison isn't feature-for-feature parity. It's whether the alternative gives every department — sales, finance, stock, manufacturing — one shared record, or whether it's a strong point solution that will need three more systems bolted around it to do the same job.
