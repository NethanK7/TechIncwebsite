Most ERP implementations that fail do not fail on technology. They fail because the whole thing was scheduled to arrive on one date, and the date arrived before the understanding did.

NXTGEN is our answer to that, developed across more than 150 implementations. It has five phases, and the second one is where most of the risk actually leaves the project.

## Design: map what happens, not what should

We map processes as they actually run — not as the org chart or the SOP says they do. That distinction produces most of the surprises, and it is much cheaper to find them now.

The output is a target state designed against Frappe's native capabilities. Where a gap is real, we plan to build for it. Where it is a habit, we say so.

## Segregate: the phase that removes the risk

Scope is split into modules that can be delivered independently, each with its own acceptance criteria.

This sounds administrative. It is the single most important structural decision in the programme, and it changes the risk profile completely:

- No module waits on every other module. Accounting can be accepted while manufacturing is still in configuration.
- A problem in one module is contained. It delays that module's gate, not the go-live.
- Progress becomes measurable in delivered function rather than in percentage-complete estimates, which are guesses.

A big-bang go-live has one failure mode and it is total. Six gated modules have six small ones.

## Cyclic Mapping: short loops, owners in the room

Configure, review, refine — with the people who own the process present, looking at the working system on their own data.

The point is not speed. It is that a misunderstood requirement surfaces in week four instead of at go-live. The cost of a requirement change rises steeply with time, and this is the mechanism that catches most of them while they are still cheap.

It also builds ownership. A process owner who has watched their workflow being shaped is a very different stakeholder from one handed a finished system.

## Training: adoption is the deliverable

Role-based training, on your data, on your workflows. Documentation of your processes, not the vendor manual.

We build internal champions deliberately, because the goal is a team that can answer its own questions. An ERP nobody uses is not an implementation, it is an expense — and the failure mode is quiet: people go back to the spreadsheet and nobody reports it.

## Go-Live Authorization: a gate, not a date

Data migration verified against source balances. Parallel runs reconciled. Sign-off from every process owner.

We do not go live on a date. We go live when the gate is genuinely clear. Saying that up front changes the conversation, because it makes readiness the shared objective rather than the calendar.

## What it adds up to

Deployment time down by up to 40% against conventional approaches, on a 12-week standard programme, with 98% client satisfaction across the portfolio.

The speed is a consequence, not the aim. Segregated scope with real gates is simply a more efficient way to work than a single monolithic push toward one immovable date.
